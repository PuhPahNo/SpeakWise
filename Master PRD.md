Speakwise Master PRD v1

1. Product Name

Speakwise

2. Product Category

Voice-first, gamified AI language-learning platform.

3. Product Thesis

Speakwise is a premium but playful AI-powered language-learning platform where users learn a new language through a personal voice-first AI tutor named Wise.

Wise remembers the learner, understands their goals and interests, adapts lessons to their progress, and turns language learning into personalized interactive missions.

Speakwise is not a generic chatbot, not a worksheet generator, and not a traditional tutoring marketplace. It is a personalized AI language coach that combines structured curriculum, voice interaction, learner memory, adaptive practice, entertainment-based learning, and gamified habit loops.

4. Core Positioning

Primary Positioning

Learn Italian with an AI tutor that remembers you.

Supporting Positioning

Speakwise turns your goals, interests, mistakes, and progress into personalized voice-first lessons.

Longer Positioning Statement

Speakwise is a voice-first AI language-learning platform for adults who want to learn Italian through personalized lessons, real-world scenarios, interactive speaking practice, adaptive correction, and a tutor that remembers how they learn.

5. Product Vision

Speakwise should feel like the Jarvis of language learning.

When a learner logs in, Wise should greet them by name, know where they left off, understand their current strengths and weaknesses, remember their interests, and recommend what to work on next.

Example experience:

“Welcome back, Anthony. Yesterday you struggled a little with direct object pronoun agreement in the passato prossimo. Since you’re preparing for food-focused travel in Italy, I built today’s 12-minute mission around ordering at a Roman trattoria. We’ll start with a listening challenge, then a speaking task, then a quick grammar recovery drill. Ready?”

The long-term product should support both:

1. Self-led learners learning without a human tutor.
2. Tutor-supported learners working with a human teacher who can assign lessons, review work, and guide progress.

However, the first strategic focus is self-led adult learners.

6. Primary Target User

Initial Target Audience

Self-led adult learners whose native language is English and whose target language is Italian.

Best Marketable Segment

Adults learning Italian for a specific real-world goal or serious fluency goal.

Examples:

* Traveling to Italy
* Preparing for extended stays abroad
* Learning for food, culture, history, art, or politics
* Reconnecting with Italian heritage
* Building conversational fluency
* Moving beyond Duolingo-style surface learning
* Wanting a more personal, interactive, voice-first learning experience

Initial Language Scope

* Native language: English
* Target language: Italian

The product architecture should support additional native and target languages later, but the first elite experience should focus on English-speaking learners of Italian.

7. Core User Promise

Emotional Promise

Practical Promise

Speakwise remembers what you know, what you struggle with, what you care about, and what you should practice next.

Product Promise

10. Main AI Character: Wise

Wise is the main interaction layer of Speakwise. Wise should feel like a personal AI language tutor, coach, and learning assistant.

* Greet the user by name
* Remember what the user did last session
* Recommend the next best lesson
* Ask what the user wants to work on
* Start a planned lesson
* Generate a new lesson on command
* Quiz the user on older vocabulary
* Explain concepts simply or deeply depending on preference
* Adjust difficulty dynamically
* Switch between voice and text modes

* Correct spoken and written responses
* Encourage the user
* Suggest recovery lessons after missed practice
* Update learner memory after each session
* Generate progress reports when asked
* Support structured learning or freestyle exploration

Possible personality modes:

* Friendly tutor
* Direct coach

11. Voice-First Experience

Voice should be the primary interface from day one.

Text mode should be available as a full fallback, not a reduced experience.

Voice Principles

* Users should be able to control the platform by speaking naturally.
* Voice should handle both navigation and learning interactions.
* Wise should speak back when voice mode is active.
* Users should be able to interrupt, pause, repeat, slow down, or change direction.
* The experience should feel like commanding a personal AI tutor, not filling out forms.

Example Voice Commands

* “Start today’s lesson.”
* “Quiz me on yesterday’s words.”
* “Explain that slower.”
* “Give me a harder version.”
* “Make this lesson about food.”
* “Switch to text mode.”
* “Only speak Italian for the next five minutes.”
* “Correct me after each sentence.”
* “Don’t interrupt me unless the mistake is major.”
* “Build a lesson for tomorrow.”
* “Give me homework.”
* “How am I doing?”
* “What should I work on next?”

12. Language Behavior

At the beginning, Wise should mostly use the user’s native language, English, while introducing Italian gradually.

As the learner progresses, Wise should increase Italian usage naturally.

Language Progression

Beginner:

* Explanations mostly in English
* Italian used in examples, vocabulary, and short prompts
* Simple spoken Italian greetings introduced gradually

Intermediate:

* More mixed Italian and English
* Wise asks simple questions in Italian
* Corrections may include Italian explanations with English support

Advanced:

* Wise can operate mostly in Italian
* English used only when helpful
* More nuanced correction of tone, idiom, register, and naturalness

Users should be able to override language behavior.

13. Learning Model

Speakwise should use a hybrid learning model:

Structured Curriculum Spine

There should be a standard Italian curriculum underneath the product.

This curriculum defines the correct order of skill development, including:

* Pronunciation basics
* Greetings and survival phrases
* Gender and number agreement
* Articles
* Present tense verbs
* Essere, avere, fare
* Common regular verbs
* Common irregular verbs
* Prepositions
* Direct object pronouns
* Indirect object pronouns
* Passato prossimo
* Imperfetto
* Future tense
* Conditional
* Subjunctive
* Listening comprehension
* Speaking fluency
* Cultural usage
* Tone and register

Dynamic Personalization Layer

The surface content of lessons should be personalized to the user’s goals and interests.

Examples:

Same grammar skill: direct object pronouns.

Food-focused learner:

“I ordered the pasta. I ate it.”

Politics-focused learner:

“I watched the debate. I understood it.”

Travel-focused learner:

“I lost the ticket. I found it.”

Culture-focused learner:

“I visited the museum. I loved it.”

User Freedom

Users should not be forced to follow the curriculum rigidly.

They can freestyle, request custom lessons, skip topics, or focus on personal goals.

However, Wise should guide them intelligently.

Example:

“We can absolutely practice restaurant conversation today. One note: you have not fully mastered articles yet, so I’ll weave in a few quick article checks as we go.”

14. Core Product Loop

The core Speakwise loop is:

1. User logs in.
2. Wise greets the user by name.
3. Wise remembers where the user left off.
4. Wise recommends a lesson, mission, drill, conversation, or recovery session.
5. User accepts, modifies, or freestyles.
6. Wise runs the session through voice and/or text.
7. User completes interactive tasks.
8. Wise corrects and encourages.
9. Speakwise updates learner memory, vocabulary, skill progress, and weak spots.
10. Wise summarizes the session.
11. Wise recommends what to do next.
12. User returns later and the experience continues from memory.

15. First-Time User Flow

Goal

Onboarding should feel like a conversation with Wise, not a boring form.

Flow

1. User creates account.
2. Wise introduces itself.
3. User chooses or confirms native language.
4. User chooses target language.
5. User describes why they want to learn.
6. User shares interests.
7. User selects or describes preferred lesson style.
8. User selects preferred Wise personality mode or keeps default.
9. User estimates current level.
10. If unsure, Wise runs a short conversational placement assessment.
11. Wise creates a learner profile.
12. Wise summarizes the profile and asks for confirmation.
13. Wise recommends the first lesson.

Onboarding Input Modes

* Voice by default
* Text fallback at every step
* Manual edit option after onboarding

Example Onboarding Interaction

Wise:

“Welcome to Speakwise. I’m Wise, your personal Italian tutor. I’ll build your lessons around your goals, interests, and progress. First, what language do you speak most comfortably?”

User:

“English.”

Wise:

“Great. And you’re learning Italian. What’s your main reason for learning?”

User:

“I’m going to Italy and want to explore the food scene.”

Wise:

“Perfect. I’ll shape your lessons around restaurants, markets, menus, kitchens, and travel situations while still teaching the grammar you need in the right order.”

18. Correction Philosophy

Corrections should be adaptive to the user.

Default correction style:

1. Encourage
2. Correct clearly
3. Explain simply
4. Give another chance
5. Save the pattern if repeated

Example Correction

User says:

“Ho visto la ieri.”

Wise responds:

“Good — your meaning was clear. One correction: in Italian, when la comes before ho visto, it becomes l’, and the past participle agrees with it. So the natural version is: L’ho vista ieri. Try saying that again.”

Correction Timing

Wise should support multiple correction modes:

* Immediate correction
* End-of-task correction
* Major mistakes only
* Fluency-first mode
* Grammar-focused mode
* Pronunciation-focused mode

The default should adapt based on activity type and user preference.

Speaking conversation should avoid constant interruption unless the user requests strict correction.

Drills should provide more immediate correction.

27. AI Behavior Rules

Wise must:

* Use learner profile before generating lessons
* Respect user preferences when possible
* Maintain curriculum awareness
* Encourage without being patronizing
* Correct accurately
* Avoid over-correcting during fluency tasks
* Update memory after meaningful sessions
* Ask clarifying questions only when necessary
* Recommend but not force learning paths
* Avoid acting like a final authority when uncertain
* Support text fallback for every voice action
* Adapt language usage to learner level

Wise must not:

* Randomly generate lessons without checking context
* Ignore user goals
* Overwhelm beginners with too much target-language explanation
* Shame users for missed practice
* Pretend the user mastered something without evidence
* Store unnecessary sensitive personal details
* Use copyrighted clips in ways that violate content rules
* Break character into generic chatbot mode unless requested

29. Major Risks

Product Risks

* Too many features create a chaotic first experience
* Voice latency makes the product feel less magical
* AI-generated curriculum becomes inconsistent
* Gamification feels childish or annoying
* Wise feels like ChatGPT with a different name
* User freedom undermines curriculum progression
* Media content introduces legal or technical complexity

Technical Risks

* Voice pipeline complexity
* Memory quality and retrieval issues
* Agent-generated code fragmentation
* Inconsistent data models across modules
* Prompt drift
* High AI/voice costs
* Poor lesson state management

Learning Risks

* Incorrect grammar explanations
* Overconfident corrections
* Poor level calibration
* Too much personalization without enough pedagogy
* User feels entertained but does not progress

33. Final Product Summary

Speakwise is a voice-first AI language-learning platform for adults learning Italian.

Its central experience is Wise, a personal AI tutor that remembers the learner, adapts to their goals and interests, guides them through a structured curriculum, and turns practice into personalized interactive missions.

The product should feel like a premium Jarvis-style language command center: intelligent, personal, responsive, encouraging, and fun without becoming childish.

The strategic wedge is not simply AI tutoring. It is personalized, voice-first language learning with persistent memory and adaptive mission-based practice.

The long-term platform can expand to more languages, tutor-supported learning, media-rich lessons, and advanced analytics, but the foundation must be a cohesive learner memory system connected to Wise, curriculum, practice, correction, and progress.

Core product idea:

Speakwise helps you learn a language with an AI tutor that remembers you.

Core experience:

Wise greets you, remembers your progress, teaches through your interests, corrects you at your level, and keeps your learning moving forward.

Core promise:

Every session becomes part of your next session.
